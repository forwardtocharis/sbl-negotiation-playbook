## 7. What was not done, and what to watch

1. **The register's `verified`, reliability and `[VERIFY]` content was not revisited.** WP12 moved
   labels, not propositions. Every review item in `docs/review_priorities.md` other than item 63 is
   still open, including the five at the top of the list.
2. **`LAW-EW-06` to `LAW-EW-09` keep sequence labels.** They do not collide today, but they are the
   remaining part of group A's sequence, so a future English source added to clauses 06, 07 or 09
   as `LAW-EW-06` would recreate exactly this problem. The subject-specific scheme
   (`LAW-EW-TA1925-S16`) is the one to extend. **Nothing enforces that**: the one-to-one check is a
   scratchpad script, not a test. If you want it permanent, it is about twenty lines in
   `tests/test_schema_validation.py` and would have caught the original collision at WP3.
3. **The README's counts are still unpinned.** `tests/test_readme.py` pins the hash only. A clause
   promoted from `stub` to `draft` changes the counts, changes the hash, and the test will fail on
   the hash, which is enough to make somebody repaste the block, but the failure message will not
   mention the counts.
4. **`docs/demo_script.md` is still unpinned in every other respect.** WP10b §5 item 5 stands: its
   floors and quoted drafting notes can be falsified by a `playbook/` edit and nothing connects the
   golden tests back to it.
5. **The snapshots now differ from WP9's and WP10b's pasted hash** in every hand-off note written
   before this one. Those notes are history and were deliberately not rewritten; `5752d729...` in
   `handoff/WP9.md` and `handoff/WP10b.md` is the correct record of what those work packages saw.
