**A caveat on that grep, and it matters.** Excluding whole lines that contain `http` is weaker than
the repo's own test. `tests/test_denylist.py` strips only the `http…` *token* and `url:` lines, then
flattens whitespace across the whole document — so a Markdown table row carrying both a firm name
and that firm's URL trips the test but not this grep, and a two-word name wrapped over a line break
trips the test but not a line-based grep either. That is exactly how three hits in
`docs/research/jurisdictions_research.md` survived my first pass. **Treat
`pytest tests/test_denylist.py` as the real check and this grep as a convenience.** Both are clean
now.

## 4. Collisions found

Group C's warning was right, and it is worse than group C could see from inside its own fragment.
**Four `LAW-EW-*` labels each name two different documents**, because group A continued the
sequence for clause 10's English trust/partnership/company sources while group C independently
continued the same sequence for the financial-collateral and enforcement sources.

| ref label | document A (clause files) | document B (clause files) |
|---|---|---|
| `LAW-EW-02` | Law of Property Act 1925 s.103 (`margin_maintenance_cure`, `enforcement_liquidation`) | FCARs 2003 reg. 4 (`conditions_precedent`) |
| `LAW-EW-03` | Trustee Act 1925 s.16 (`capacity_authority`) | FCARs 2003 reg. 4 (`security_structure_control`) |
| `LAW-EW-04` | Trustee Act 2000 s.31 (`capacity_authority`) | FCARs 2003 reg. 17 as made (`enforcement_liquidation`) |
| `LAW-EW-05` | Partnership Act 1890 s.5 (`capacity_authority`) | FCARs 2003 reg. 18 as made (`enforcement_liquidation`) |

There is also a knock-on: **FCARs reg. 4 is cited under two different labels** (`LAW-EW-02` in
clause 02, `LAW-EW-03` in clause 07), which is the other face of the same problem. Fifteen documents
in total are cited under more than one label; the other fourteen are harmless aliases where a clause
file's short code meets the descriptive label used in the jurisdiction, flag and rule files, and
they are tabulated for completeness.

`FIRM-04` and `FIRM-05`, the labels group C flagged as the likely collision, **do not collide** —
group A used `FIRM-TR-01`, not `FIRM-04`.

**Nothing was renamed**, per instruction: the labels sit inside `sources` entries and in `note`
prose other agents wrote, and renaming from here would be editing their work on a guess. The
register records both sides with the clause files on each, and suggests a resolution (keep group
C's sequence, which is used across three files and matches the `UK-FCAR-2003` rows, and move clause
10's five sources onto subject-specific labels, which cannot collide with a sequence). That touches
two clause files and would fix the two-labels-one-document case at the same time.

## 5. Lowest-confidence items

The full list is `docs/review_priorities.md` — 63 items in three bands, ordered by whether the item
fires on a golden deal, moves a floor, or is text only. The band assignments are derived from the
actual run outputs of all six deals rather than from the plan's table. The top of the list:

1. `playbook/flags/regulatory_flags.yaml` `consumer_credit.description` — the UK exemption threshold
   figures, on the flag that fires on **four of the six** golden deals.
2. `playbook/jurisdictions/luxembourg.yaml` `financial_collateral_regime` — natural persons in scope
   is an inference from two provisions, not an express scope article. WP7's own highest priority,
   and the contrast that justifies printing a jurisdiction overlay at all.
3. `playbook/rules/rules.yaml` `R06`/`R06b` thresholds of 25 and 50 per cent — unobserved anywhere,
   and between them they tighten two floors and escalate to credit on D02.
4. `playbook/rules/rules.yaml` `R01_trust_borrower` — the indemnity analysis has no source; WP5b's
   own number one.
5. `playbook/clauses/10_capacity_authority.yaml` `positions.fallback` — full limited recourse; WP3
   group A's least-confident position anywhere, and reversing it makes two `set_floor` actions
   inert.
6. `playbook/clauses/09_enforcement_liquidation.yaml` `positions.acceptable` — commercially
   reasonable standard paired with the pre-agreed acknowledgements; group C calls it the position
   most needing a lawyer rather than a credit officer.

Findings from building the list that are worth flagging on their own:

- **Three rules never fire on any golden deal**: `R05b_third_party_corporate_pledgor`,
  `R08_restricted_securities`, `R15_illiquid_collateral`. Each raises floors when it does fire, and
  R08 and the `restricted_securities` flag are both built on a US resale rule in a playbook whose
  jurisdictions are England, Switzerland and Luxembourg. They are untested in practice as well as
  unsourced.
- **Four flags are never raised** on any golden deal: `emir_awareness` (referenced by nothing at
  all, so it can never appear in any output), `prudential_awareness` (referenced by three clauses
  but by no rule), `restricted_securities` and `financial_assistance_corporate_benefit`.
- `consumer_credit` and `fcar_safe_harbour_uncertain` are by far the most load-bearing flags, at
  four and three deals respectively, and both carry `[VERIFY]` on the part that matters.

### Design questions, recorded in §"Design questions" of `docs/review_priorities.md`

1. **Jurisdiction-dependent openings and a possible `jurisdiction_overrides` field.** Clause 07's
   `fallback` and clause 09's `preferred` are genuinely jurisdiction-dependent. The frozen schema
   has one ladder per clause, so group C put the constraint in `jurisdiction_notes` and in the
   tier's `conditions`. This is the one place a reader of the markup could take the printed opening
   and be wrong on a Swiss deal.
2. **`set_opening` is a no-op.** The opening starts at `preferred` and actions only tighten, so no
   rule can move it; D02's log prints `R06b_concentration_severe (no change)` in so many words.
   Delete it, keep it for the log, or start the opening at `default_floor` — a contract decision,
   not an engine one.
3. **What "touched" should mean** for the run's not-automated list. Currently: a rule touched the
   clause, **or** an escalation names it, **or** one of its `regulatory_flags` was raised. The third
   limb is wide — on D02 it yields more than twenty items, which risks becoming wallpaper.
4. **The `[VERIFY]` count.** 101 occurrences (45 clauses, 39 rules, 10 flags, 7 jurisdictions)
   against 20 `verified: false` source entries and 243 `verified: true`. Every one is deliberate and
   every one prints. Where is the line between scrupulous and not ready to be shown.
5. **Approver names are free strings** in `Position.approval`, `EscalateAction.to` and
   `Flag.who_decides`, and the vocabulary is already drifting. WP1 raised it; it is the single most
   important field in the model.
